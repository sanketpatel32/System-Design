# SLA (Service Level Agreement)
> **Category:** Observability

---

### Overview
A **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** is a legally binding or formal commercial contract between a service provider and its customers. It defines expected performance standards (derived from SLOs) and explicitly specifies financial penalties, service credits, or remedies if those standards are breached.

### Comparison: SLI vs SLO vs SLA

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                          Service Reliability Pyramid                       |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  [ SLA ]  Legal & Financial Contract (e.g., 99.5% Uptime -> 10% Credit)    |
|  [ SLO ]  Internal Target Goal (e.g., 99.9% Uptime over 30 days)         |
|  [ SLI ]  Real-time Metric Measurement (e.g., HTTP 200 Ratio = 99.94%)      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Strategic Alignment Matrix

| Feature | SLI | SLO | SLA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary Audience** | Site Reliability Engineers / Devs | Engineering Management & Product | Business Executives, Legal, Customers |
| **Definition** | Specific metric measurement | Internal target percentage | External binding commercial agreement |
| **Consequence of Breach** | Triggers monitoring alerts | Consumes Error Budget; freezes deploys | Financial penalty, service credits, refund |
| **Typical Threshold** | N/A (continuous value) | Stricter (e.g., 99.9%) | More lenient (e.g., 99.5%) |

### Standard SLA Financial Penalty Structure

| Monthly Uptime Achieved | SLA Status | Customer Remedy / Service Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **\(\ge 99.9\%\)** | Compliant | Standard Billing (0% credit) |
| **99.0% - 99.89%** | Minor Breach | 10% Service Credit on Monthly Invoice |
| **95.0% - 98.99%** | Moderate Breach | 25% Service Credit on Monthly Invoice |
| **< 95.0%** | Severe Breach | 50% - 100% Service Credit / Termination Rights |

### Key Architecture & Contract Considerations
1. **Exclusions**: SLAs must exclude downtime caused by customer misconfiguration, internet backbone outages, force majeure, or scheduled maintenance.
2. **Measurement Window**: Clearly state whether the SLA is measured monthly, quarterly, or annually.
3. **Safety Margins**: Never copy internal SLOs directly into SLAs; enforce a buffer (e.g., SLO = 99.9%, SLA = 99.5%).

### Key takeaway
An **SLA** converts engineering reliability into business and legal commitments. Protect business margin by keeping SLAs looser than internal SLOs and establishing strict exclusion clauses for third-party infrastructure failures.
