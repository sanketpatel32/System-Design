# SLA (Service Level Agreement)
> **Category:** Observability

---

### Overview
A **Service Level Agreement (SLA)** is a legally binding or contractual agreement between a service provider and its customers that explicitly defines guaranteed performance standards (usually derived from internal SLOs) and details financial penalties, service credits, or remedies if the provider fails to meet those standards.

To safeguard against breach payouts, organizations set internal SLOs significantly tighter than external SLAs (e.g., an internal SLO of 99.9% vs an external SLA guarantee of 99.5%).

### SLA vs SLO vs SLI Comparison Topology

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  SLI (Service Level Indicator)                                           |
|  Actual observed performance ratio (e.g., 99.94% latency < 200ms)         |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                    |
                                    v
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  SLO (Service Level Objective)                                           |
|  Internal engineering goal & error budget trigger (e.g., 99.90%)         |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                    |
                                    v
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  SLA (Service Level Agreement)                                           |
|  External customer contract with financial credits (e.g., 99.50%)        |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Financial Penalty & Credit Calculation Matrix

| Monthly Uptime Percentage | Service Level Compliance | Customer Financial Credit (%) | Action / Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **≥ 99.9%** | SLA Met | 0% (Standard Billing) | No financial penalty. |
| **99.0% - 99.89%** | SLA Tier 1 Breach | 10% Service Credit | Credit applied to next month's invoice upon claim. |
| **95.0% - 98.99%** | SLA Tier 2 Breach | 25% Service Credit | Mandatory root-cause analysis (RCA) delivered to customer. |
| **< 95.0%** | SLA Tier 3 Breach | 50% - 100% Refund | Executive escalation & contract termination clause option. |

### Comparison Matrix: SLA vs SLO vs SLI

| Characteristic | SLI | SLO | SLA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Target Audience** | SRE & Infrastructure Engineers | Product & Software Developers | Enterprise Customers & Legal Teams |
| **Primary Goal** | Real-time operational measurement | Engineering prioritization & release pace | Risk management & financial liability |
| **Strictness Level** | High precision (Raw Telemetry) | Stricter than SLA (Safeguard margin) | Lenient compared to SLO (Financial safety net)|
| **Penalty for Breach**| Alert fires on dashboard | Feature deployment freeze | Cash refund or monthly invoice credit |

### Architectural Trade-offs

| Strategy / Choice | Advantages | Disadvantages | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative SLA (e.g., 99.5% for 99.9% SLO)**| Provides substantial safety buffer against unexpected cloud outages or vendor failures. | May lose enterprise deals to competitors claiming aggressive SLAs. | SaaS products relying on public cloud infrastructure. |
| **Aggressive SLA (e.g., 99.99%)** | Key marketing differentiator for high-enterprise sales. | High financial risk of penalty payouts during major cloud region failures. | Financial payment gateways, mission-critical telco infrastructure. |
| **Automated SLA Credit Crediting** | Builds high customer trust and transparency. | Can cause unexpected revenue leakage during unexpected incident waves. | Premium developer platform infrastructure APIs. |

### Key takeaway
An **SLA** is a business and legal commitment specifying the financial consequences of downtime. Engineering teams protect the business against SLA penalties by maintaining tighter internal SLOs and continuously monitoring real-time SLIs.
